Why it matters

Four ways a tender spec goes wrong. Each one caught.

The IS catalogue runs past 20,000 documents with overlapping scopes and frequent revisions. Keyword search leaves the same gaps every time.

Superseded citations

An old revision gets copied from last year's tender and nobody notices until a bidder objects.

Currency check flags it and names the replacing standard.

Missing allied standards

The product spec is cited, but not the test method, installation code or safety standard that goes with it.

Six-way allied graph: normative, test, terminology, safety, installation, product.

Wrong document type

"Shall conform to" a test method: it describes how to test, not what to supply, so it can't be enforced.

Document-type classifier warns and suggests the product spec.

Unclear certification

Is the ISI mark mandatory under a QCO? Is it CRS? Hallmarking? Getting it wrong changes what proof you can demand.

Scheme-aware badges plus ready-to-paste clause wording.

Under the hood

        
Meaning, not keywords. And no black box.

        
A transparent retrieval pipeline that runs in milliseconds, entirely offline. Every recommendation shows the terms that matched.

      

      

        
1
Understand
Tokenises English and Devanagari (हिंदी, मराठी) queries.

        
2
Expand
Concept lexicon maps "सरिया" and "TMT" to reinforcement steel.

        
3
Score
Field-weighted IDF: title ×3, category ×2, scope ×1.5.

        
4
Re-rank
Product specs rise above test methods unless you asked about testing.

        
5
Enrich
Allied graph, currency check, amendments and certification scheme.
